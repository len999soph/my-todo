# 8th Grade Social Studies — Your Year at a Glance 🗺️

This is the New York State story of US History that JJMS follows. **7th grade**
ended around the Civil War. **8th grade starts right after it and goes to today.**

Think of it as one long story with cause-and-effect: each unit *causes* the next.

---

## Unit 8.1 — Reconstruction (1865–1877)
**The big question:** After the Civil War, how do you rebuild the country and bring
4 million freed people into American life as citizens?
- The South is destroyed; the Union has to be put back together.
- **13th, 14th, 15th Amendments** end slavery, grant citizenship, grant Black men
  the vote.
- **Freedmen's Bureau** helps formerly enslaved people.
- It ends in 1877 — and the South brings in **Jim Crow** laws and segregation.
- **Key terms:** Reconstruction, amendment, citizenship, sharecropping, Jim Crow,
  segregation, Black Codes.

## Unit 8.2 — A Changing Society: Industrialization (1865–1900)
**The big question:** How did factories, railroads, and millions of immigrants turn
America into an industrial giant — and who paid the price?
- The **Industrial Revolution** booms: railroads, steel, oil, electricity.
- **Big business** & "captains of industry" (Carnegie, Rockefeller).
- **Immigration** surges (Ellis Island); cities explode (**urbanization**).
- Workers organize into **labor unions** for better pay and safety.
- **Key terms:** industrialization, urbanization, immigration, monopoly, labor
  union, tenement, assembly line, push/pull factors.

## Unit 8.3 — The Progressive Era (1890–1920)
**The big question:** Once the problems of the industrial age appear, who fixes them?
- **Progressives** = reformers fixing corruption, unsafe food/work, child labor.
- **Muckrakers** (journalists) expose problems (e.g., *The Jungle*).
- Reforms: food safety laws, women's suffrage (**19th Amendment**, 1920).
- **Key terms:** progressive, muckraker, suffrage, reform, trust-busting.

## Unit 8.4 — The Rise of American Power (1890–1920)
**The big question:** How did the US become a world power?
- **Imperialism** — the US expands overseas.
- **Spanish-American War** (1898) → US gains territory.
- **World War I** (US enters 1917) — "make the world safe for democracy."
- **Key terms:** imperialism, isolationism, alliance, neutrality, Treaty of Versailles.

## Unit 8.5 — Prosperity & Depression (1920–1939)
**The big question:** How does the country go from the booming 1920s to the worst
economic disaster in its history?
- **Roaring Twenties:** jazz, cars, Harlem Renaissance, stock market boom.
- **Stock Market Crash (1929)** → the **Great Depression**.
- **FDR's New Deal** — government programs to fight the Depression.
- **Key terms:** Great Depression, stock market, New Deal, unemployment, Dust Bowl.

## Unit 8.6 — World War II (1939–1945)
**The big question:** Why did the US fight, and how did the war change America?
- **Pearl Harbor (1941)** pulls the US in.
- The Holocaust; D-Day; the atomic bomb.
- The home front: women in factories, Japanese American internment.
- **Key terms:** dictatorship, Holocaust, home front, internment, atomic bomb.

## Unit 8.7 — Foreign Policy & the Cold War (1945–1991)
**The big question:** How do the US and the Soviet Union compete without direct war?
- **Cold War:** capitalism vs. communism, an arms race, the **Space Race**.
- Korea & Vietnam; the **Cuban Missile Crisis**.
- **Key terms:** Cold War, communism, containment, arms race, superpower.

## Unit 8.8 — A World in Uncertain Times (1991–today)
**The big question:** What does the US look like after the Cold War ends?
- Globalization, the internet, **9/11** and the War on Terror.
- **Key terms:** globalization, terrorism, technology.

## Unit 8.9 — Domestic Politics & Reform: Civil Rights
**The big question:** How did Americans fight to make "all men are created equal"
actually true?
- The **Civil Rights Movement** (MLK, Rosa Parks, *Brown v. Board*).
- Later movements: women's rights, and others expanding equality.
- **Key terms:** civil rights, segregation, boycott, civil disobedience, Supreme Court.

## Geography skills woven through the whole year
- Reading and comparing **maps** (political, physical, thematic).
- **Regions** of the US and the world.
- **Push & pull factors** of migration (why people leave / why they come).
- How **geography shapes history** (rivers → cities, resources → industry).

## How NY tests you
NY social studies tests mix:
- **Multiple choice** (recall + understanding)
- **Document-Based Questions (DBQs)** — read a source and explain it
- **Short answers / essays** — explain cause and effect in your own words

The tutor will practice all of these with you. Start with `articles/01-reconstruction.md`.
